\section{Purpose}

The pass of \code{JScan} (\code{jscan\_doc.tex}) reads a run once and
writes, per event, the corrected summed waveform, the charge and time of
every channel in a window, and the single-photoelectron candidates.
\code{offline.py} does the analysis of one run from that file: it
classifies the events, computes the charge of each event in seven ways,
fits the 511~keV photopeak of each, fits the decay of the average pulse,
and writes a per-event table and a summary for the comparison across runs
(\code{scan.py}). It reads only the pass output and the equalization table,
runs in seconds, and is a batch script: no steps, one table on the
terminal, six figures. The definitions of the classes and of the charges
are those of \code{selection\_doc.tex} (steps 6 to 12); this document
gives what is new: the charge noise from the data, the equalized charges,
the photopeak and decay fits, the output.

\section{Per event}

$S_i$ is the stored summed waveform divided by its scale; $\Delta t$ = 8~ns;
$a$, $b$ the window in samples from \code{--window}.

\paragraph{Pulse and class.} $\hat S$ = moving average of $S$ over $N$
samples; $i_{\max}$, $H$, $i_{\rm rise}$ as in \code{selection\_doc.tex}
step~7. Charge window $[i_{\max} - a, i_{\max} + b]$ and control window
of the same length in the pulse-free part (step~8). The baseline of the sum is the
end-of-record baseline of the pass. A local baseline was tried (the
median of a post-pulse region 8 to 10~\us after the maximum, and a line
from a pre-trigger level to it) and does not help: the sum wanders by
about 13 counts rms over the 5~\us of the charge window itself, so a
baseline next to the pulse leaves as much wander in the charge as one far
from it; and the samples just before the window cannot be used at all,
they hold the trigger-time excursion. The charge noise of the sum,
$\sigma_Q$, is 12\% of the 511~keV peak for this reason, and $Q$ of the
sum is not the estimator to use for the resolution; the per-channel
estimators that sum only the channels with light carry a tenth of that
noise. The reason: every channel has a
slow wander of about 0.7 counts rms at the microsecond scale, only a tenth
of it common to the channels (\code{chanstat.py}), so no common-mode
estimate removes it, and the end-of-record baseline of the pass, 20~\us
away, leaves it in the charge: the charge noise of the sum was then 12\%
of the 511~keV peak. A baseline next to the pulse removes everything slower
than a few microseconds. The samples just before the charge window cannot
be used: they hold the trigger-time excursion. $Q = \Delta t \sum_{\rm
window} S_i$, $Q_{\rm ctrl}$ the same over the control window. The charge
noise is measured on the run: $\sigma_Q$ = rms of $Q_{\rm ctrl}$ over all
events (clipped at three times a first rms, so that pulses in the control
window do not inflate it); this replaces the white-noise formula of the
prototype. $\sigma_N$ = rms of $\hat S$ over the control window, per event.
Class: EMPTY if $H < K\sigma_N$; REAL if $H \ge K\sigma_N$ and a rising
edge; TAIL if $H \ge K\sigma_N$ and no rising edge. The class rests on the
height of the smoothed pulse against the smoothed noise, not on $Q$
against $\sigma_Q$ as in the prototype: $\sigma_Q$ is dominated by the
slow wander of the sum, and a cut on $Q$ at five times it reaches into the
511~keV peak. Pile-up: the maximum
$P_2$ of $\hat S$ outside the charge window extended by the veto
(\code{--veto}) on each side, the valley $V$ between it and $i_{\max}$;
flagged if $P_2 > K\sigma_N$ and $P_2 - V > K\sigma_N$ (step~10); $\Delta
t_{\rm pile}$ = time of $P_2$ minus $t_{\max}$. The veto was added after
the first run: without it the slow tail and the rise of the main pulse,
just outside the window, were flagged as second maxima and piled up at the
window edges in the $\Delta t_{\rm pile}$ histogram.

\paragraph{Charges from the channels.} $q_c$ and $q_c^{\rm ctrl}$ are the
stored per-channel charges in the window of the pass. The charge noise of
channel $c$ is $\sigma_{q,c}$ = clipped rms of $q_c^{\rm ctrl}$ over the
events. Dead channels are removed. The equalization factor $g_c$ comes
from the table; a live channel without one gets $g_c = 1$ and is listed in
the output. With $L$ the live channels,
\[
  Q_{\rm all} = \sum_{c \in L} q_c, \qquad
  Q_0 = \sum_{c \in L,\, q_c > 0} q_c, \qquad
  Q_{\rm kept} = \sum_{c \in L,\, q_c > k\,\sigma_{q,c}} q_c,
\]
and the same three with $q_c / g_c$ in place of $q_c$ (the thresholds on
the unequalized $q_c$, so that the two sets select the same channels):
$Q^{\rm eq}_{\rm all}$, $Q^{\rm eq}_0$, $Q^{\rm eq}_{\rm kept}$. The
multiplicity $M$ is the number of live channels with $q_c > K\sigma_{q,c}$.

\section{Per run}

\paragraph{Classes.} EMPTY (no pulse above the cut), TAIL (no rising
edge), SHORT (the charge window starts before the record or its
after-window ends after it: the flag \code{short} of the pass; in older
passes with the adjacent baseline, no room for the full pre-window or the
after-window; before that, never) and REAL, decided in that order.
The fraction of each, the
fraction of REAL events with pile-up, and the histogram of $\Delta t_{\rm
pile}$ (figure \code{pileup}): a pile-up of random second pulses is flat
in $\Delta t$; a pile of entries just outside the window would be the tail
of the main pulse passing as a second one.

\paragraph{Spectra and photopeak.} For the REAL events without pile-up,
the histogram of each of the eight charges ($Q$, the six from the
channels and $Q^{\rm pos}_0$ below), 200 bins from zero to the 99.5th percentile. The photopeak is
located as the highest maximum of the smoothed histogram: the 511~keV
peak dominates the spectrum, the Compton continuum lies below it and the
1275~keV line is weaker (a rule that took the rightmost maximum above a
quarter of the highest picked the 1275~keV bump in small samples). It is fitted with a Gaussian on a background,
\[
  f(x) = n\, e^{-(x - \mu)^2 / 2 s^2} + c_0\, \tfrac{1}{2}\operatorname{erfc}\!\big(\tfrac{x - \mu}{s\sqrt 2}\big) + c_1,
\]
the erfc term being the step of the Compton continuum under the peak,
over $\mu \pm 2.5 s$, twice (the range updated after a first fit), by
least squares with Poisson weights. The starting width is the width of
the smoothed histogram at half its height around the located peak (a
fixed 8\% of the position, used first, made the fit lock on a narrow
slice of peaks that are 40\% wide), and the mean and width are bounded
to within one starting width of it; a fit whose error is infinite or
above 20\% of the position has reached a bound and is rejected. Light yield of the estimator: $\mu$
in ADC\,ns, and in photoelectrons $\mu / q_{\rm pe}$ with $q_{\rm pe} = A_1
\sum_j T_j \Delta t$ the charge of one photoelectron, $A_1$ the median
single-photoelectron amplitude of the gain table and $T$ the template of
the pass; $A_1$ is measured at 41~V only, and the conversion is labelled
with it. Resolution: $\mathrm{FWHM}/\mu = 2.355\, s / \mu$. Figure
\code{spectra}: the seven spectra with their fits; figure \code{classes}:
the $Q$ spectrum of each class.

\paragraph{Where the light fell: the seed channel.} The seed channel of
an event is the live channel with the largest equalized charge, a proxy
for the position of the interaction; its distance to the nearest dead
channel on the map (\code{src/channel\_map.csv}) is $d_{\rm dead}$. For
the REAL events without pile-up, the $Q^{\rm eq}_0$ photopeak is fitted in
three bands of $d_{\rm dead}$ (on the block, next to it, far) and in three
bands of multiplicity and two of $t_{\max}$. Then the events are grouped
by seed channel and, for each channel with at least 40 events, the
photopeak of the group is located on $Q_0$ and on $Q^{\rm eq}_0$ (the
mode of the smoothed histogram, 60 bins; the width from the fit when the
group has 100 events, from the smoothed histogram otherwise). The
terminal gives, for each of the two charges, the median position, the
spread of the positions (rms over median) and the range, and the median
and range of the widths; then one line per seed channel, ordered by
position: events, the $Q_0$ and $Q^{\rm eq}_0$ positions relative to
their medians, the gain factor of the channel and the $Q^{\rm eq}_0$
width. Comparing the spread on $Q_0$ and on $Q^{\rm eq}_0$ says whether
the gain table moves the peaks; a spread that follows the map and not the
gain factors is light collection. Figure \code{seedmap}: the position, the
width and the number of events on the map of the channels.

\paragraph{Response correction by seed channel.} The eighth charge,
$Q^{\rm pos}_0 = Q^{\rm eq}_0 / f_{\rm seed}$, divides the charge of the
event by the $Q^{\rm eq}_0$ photopeak position of its seed channel
relative to the median of the positions, taken from the groups above
(events whose seed channel has fewer than 40 events are left
uncorrected; their number is printed). It is fitted, written and plotted
like the other seven, and the splits by multiplicity and $t_{\max}$ are
printed for $Q^{\rm eq}_0$ and for $Q^{\rm pos}_0$. The factors are
measured on the run itself, with the events of the run: a mild
overfit (one parameter per 40 events at least) and, when the spectrum
holds two lines of similar weight, a meaningless one, since the located
peak of a group may be either line (196K\_39d5V\_th20, 2026-10-15).

\paragraph{The pre-window.} The per-channel baseline of the pass uses
the pre-window the record has, up to its full length $n_b$
(\code{nb\_used}, with $n_b$ from the attribute \code{adj\_before\_us}).
The REAL events without pile-up are split into those with the full
pre-window, those with a partial one and those without (after-window
alone), and the photopeak of $Q^{\rm eq}_0$ and $Q^{\rm pos}_0$ is fitted
in each group: the positions must agree, since the groups differ only in
the baseline and in the time of the trigger. One line per group on the
terminal, with the number of events and the peak with its error; figure
\code{prewindow}: the three spectra of each charge with their fits.

\paragraph{Average pulse and decay time.} The mean of $S$ over the REAL
events without pile-up, each shifted so that its $i_{\max}$ falls on a
common sample, over the samples all of them cover. The decay is fitted
between $t_{\max} + A$ and $t_{\max} + B$ (\code{--decay-range}) with two
exponentials, $A_1 e^{-t/\tau_1} + A_2 e^{-t/\tau_2} + C$, $\tau_1 <
\tau_2$ (\code{--decay-model 2}, the default: the average pulse is not a
single exponential, it decays fast first and slowly after), or with one
(\code{--decay-model 1}). The fraction of the light in the fast component
is $f_1 = A_1 \tau_1 / (A_1 \tau_1 + A_2 \tau_2)$. When one component
carries more than 95\% of the light, or the two time constants agree
within 10\%, or the error of either is larger than half its value, the two
exponentials are degenerate and the single-exponential fit is reported
instead, with \code{decay\_model} = 1 in the summary and a line on the
terminal saying so. The decay time of the
run reported to \code{scan.py} is $\tau_2$, the slow component, with
$\tau_1$ and $f_1$ beside it. Figure \code{pulse}: the average pulse and
the fit, linear and logarithmic.

\section{Output}

\paragraph{Noise of each estimator.} The same sum that defines an
estimator, taken over the control charges of the pass with the masks of
the event (the positive channels, the kept channels, the gain factors,
the seed-channel factor), is the charge of an empty window with the
estimator's own selection; its clipped rms over the selected events with
a valid control window is the noise of the estimator, printed in
ADC\,ns, as the FWHM term $2.355\,\sigma/\mu$, and as the width with that
term subtracted in quadrature.

\paragraph{Table.} One line per estimator: position (ADC\,ns and
photoelectrons), error, width, FWHM/position, number of events in the
fit, noise, noise term and width after it; then the class fractions, the
pile-up fraction, $\sigma_Q$, the decay time, and the channels without an
equalization factor.

\paragraph{Per-event file} \code{pass/RUN\_offline.h5}: datasets of length
nev: \code{index}, \code{cls} (0 EMPTY, 1 REAL, 2 TAIL, 3 SHORT), \code{pileup},
\code{dt\_pile}, \code{t\_max}, \code{H}, \code{t\_rise}, \code{Q},
\code{Q\_ctrl}, \code{Q\_all}, \code{Q\_0}, \code{Q\_kept},
\code{Q\_all\_eq}, \code{Q\_0\_eq}, \code{Q\_kept\_eq}, \code{Q\_0\_pos},
\code{mult}, \code{seed}, \code{d\_dead}; the
average pulse as \code{pulse} with its time axis; the options and the
input files as attributes.

\paragraph{Summary} \code{pass/RUN\_offline.json}: the run conditions
copied from the pass (T1, T2, T3, bias, trigger rate), the options, the
class fractions, $\sigma_Q$, the decay time with its error, for each
estimator the photopeak position, error, width, resolution and noise, and
the photopeak of each pre-window group (\code{prewindow}). This is what
\code{scan.py} reads.

\section{\code{mains.py}: is the baseline drift the power line?}

\begin{lstlisting}
python3 src/py/mains.py RUN|FILE [--freq 50] [--save NAME]
\end{lstlisting}
Reads the pass output: the end-of-record baseline of the sum of every
event and the trigger time. Folds the baseline at the period of the
mains (20~ms for 50~Hz) and fits the first three harmonics; prints the
scatter of the baseline between events (the size of the slow drift), the
amplitudes and the scatter left. Figure: the baseline against the phase
with the median per phase bin, and against the time in the run. On the
196~K bias scan (2026-10-15) no harmonic is seen, and the scatter falls
from 47 counts at 41~V to 5.6 at 39~V, following the square root of the
board trigger rate (\code{ANALYSIS.md}).
