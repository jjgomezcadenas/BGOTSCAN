\section{Purpose}

The SiPMs (Hamamatsu S14160-6050HS, read out passively into the 100~$\Omega$
input of the digitizer) produce dark pulses of one photoelectron at tens of
kHz per channel, visible in the records as isolated pulses of about 15
counts lasting 100 to 200~ns. Their amplitude, or better their charge, is
the gain of the channel in that run. It is needed to equalize the 64
channels before summing, to convert the light yield to photoelectrons, and,
through its dependence on bias, to measure the breakdown voltage at each
temperature. There is no dedicated SPE run, so the pulses are taken from the
pulse-free part of the scintillation records.

This script is the prototype of the SPE pass of \code{JScan}: it processes
one event at a time, in six steps, printing the numbers of each step and
showing one figure, so that the threshold, the smoothing and the charge
window can be judged on real pulses before they are frozen. The
definitions below are the ones the pass will implement. It is a Python
script in \code{src/py/}, reading the raw HDF5 files with h5py, and it
reuses the run lookup and the moving average of \code{wfview.py}.

\section{Usage}

\begin{lstlisting}
python3 src/py/spe.py RUN [options]
\end{lstlisting}

Interactive: after each step the script waits on the terminal; Enter goes
to the next step, \code{n} abandons the event and starts the next one,
\code{q} quits.

\section{Notation}

$w_{c,i}$ is the raw ADC value of channel $c$ at sample $i$ ($i = 0 \dots
n_s - 1$, $n_s = 3000$, $\Delta t = 8$~ns); $t_i = (i -
\code{pretrigger})\,\Delta t$ as in \code{wfview}. No common-mode
subtraction is applied: the common mode is below one count and the pulses
are fifteen.

\section{The steps}

\subsection{Step 1: read the channel}

The channel waveform minus its own baseline, $x_i = w_{c,i} - b_c$, with
$b_c$ the median of $w_c$ over the record. The search region $R$ is the
set of samples where no scintillation light is expected: with
\code{tail}, the last $T$~\us{} of the record (the pulse is near the
trigger and over well before, except in pile-up events, which are a small
contamination); with \code{all}, every sample (only meaningful for records
without scintillation light).

Text: $b_c$, the rms of $x$ over the record, the region in ns and samples.
Figure: $x$ over the record with $R$ shaded.

\subsection{Step 2: smooth and find candidates}

$\hat x$ is the moving average of $x$ of width $N$ (\code{--smooth},
default 3 samples, 24~ns: enough to take the single-sample noise down
without flattening a pulse of 100~ns). A candidate is a local maximum of
$\hat x$ inside $R$ with $\hat x > A_{\rm thr}$ (\code{--threshold}, 10
counts, 2.5 times the noise). Isolation: candidates are taken in
decreasing order of $\hat x$, and a candidate closer than the charge
window length ($A + B$) to one already taken is dropped, so that two
overlapping pulses are not counted twice and the charge of one does not
include the other.

Text: the number of candidates, their times and smoothed heights. Figure:
$x$ and $\hat x$ over $R$, the threshold line, the candidates marked.

\subsection{Step 3: amplitude and charge}

For each candidate at sample $i_0$: the amplitude $A$ is the maximum of
the raw $x$ within $\pm N$ samples of $i_0$, at sample $i_A$; the charge
is
\[
  q = \Delta t \sum_{i = i_A - a}^{i_A + b} x_i \quad (\text{ADC}\cdot\text{ns}),
  \qquad a = A_{\rm ns}/\Delta t,\; b = B_{\rm ns}/\Delta t,
\]
with the window of \code{--window} (40~ns before, 200~ns after). The
charge is the better estimator of the single photoelectron: the amplitude
is one sample, 15 counts against 4 of noise; the charge integrates about
12 effective samples of signal against noise growing as the square root
of the 30 samples of the window.

Text: one line per candidate: time, $A$, $q$, $q/A$. Figure: up to six
candidates zoomed, one panel each, with the charge window shaded and
$i_A$ marked.

\subsection{Step 4: all 64 channels}

Steps 1 to 3 applied to every channel. Text: the number of candidates per
channel as an $8\times8$ table in channel order, the total and the mean
per channel. Figure: the $8\times8$ grid of the channels over $R$ with
their candidates marked.

\subsection{Step 5: rates}

The dark rate of channel $c$ in this event is $n_c / T_R$, with $n_c$ its
number of candidates and $T_R$ the length of $R$ in seconds. Over one
event this is a Poisson count of a few; the map over many events is the
dark-count map of the run. Text: the mean rate and the channels more than
three standard deviations from it. Figure: the $8\times8$ map of $n_c /
T_R$.

\subsection{Step 6: accumulated spectra}

Histograms of $q$ and of $A$ over all events processed so far, for the
selected channel and for all channels together. The expected shape: a
peak at the single-photoelectron charge $q_1$, a smaller peak near $2
q_1$ from the pulses with a crosstalk partner (7\% on the datasheet), and
a tail from pulses that overlapped. The threshold removes the low side of
the first peak and must sit well below it, which is what this figure
checks. The estimate of $q_1$ is the mode of the $q$ histogram (and of
$A$ for $A_1$); the crosstalk check is the number of pulses between
$1.5\,q_1$ and $2.5\,q_1$ divided by the number below $1.5\,q_1$.

Text: the number of pulses, the modes, the crosstalk ratio, for the
channel and for all channels. Figure: the four histograms with the modes
marked.

\section{Modes}

Interactive, as \code{selection.py}: one prompt per step. With
\code{--from-step 6} steps 1 to 5 are silent and there is one prompt per
event, showing the growing spectra. With \code{--save NAME} there are no
prompts and every figure is saved. With \code{--summary} nothing is shown
or prompted per event; after the last event the step~6 text is printed
and its figure shown, or saved as \code{NAME\_spe.png} with \code{--save}.
This is the mode for a few hundred events.

\section{What the pass will do with it}

The SPE pass of \code{JScan} applies steps 1 to 3 to every channel of
every event and writes one row per candidate (event, channel, time, $A$,
$q$). The offline analysis then histograms $q$ per channel and per run,
takes $q_1$ as the gain, derives the equalization constants $g_c = q_1(c)
/ \langle q_1 \rangle$ for the selection pass, the gain versus bias at
185 and 196~K (a straight line in overvoltage whose zero is the breakdown
voltage), the gain versus temperature along the scan, and the dark-count
rate per channel. Not in this script: fitting the peaks, a search region
defined from the pulse of the sum, and any output file.
